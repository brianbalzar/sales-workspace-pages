## Hard rules for the call script

Everything in quotes is written to be spoken: contractions, short sentences, fragments are fine. Round numbers the way a person would ("a couple hundred coolers," not "180"). Exact figures live in Prep only. The sender's callback number is {{phone}}.

- Prep, reference lightly: 3–5 facts the sender can mention without sounding rehearsed. Exact figures here.
- Prep, don't bring up: 3–6 items from the research, each with a few words on why. Always include the recipient's title if the research shows any inconsistency, and any vendors, engineers, or consultants named.
- Opening: one sentence plus a permission question. Under 30 words. Full name and Upchurch. The motive lands here.
- The 30 seconds: 60–80 words. The detail, the timing problem, the work, the ask, in that order.
- If asked what Upchurch does: under 30 words. The company in one clause, the sender's department's work in one sentence.
- Discovery: exactly three open questions, each built from the chosen detail or timing problem, each answerable by someone who's never heard of Upchurch. No question that could be asked of any facilities director anywhere.
- Objections: the two most likely given the research and reason for outreach. Each response under 30 words: one sentence that reframes, one that asks. No arguing.
- Close: one sentence. Specific duration of 20 minutes or less, offer to come to them.
- Voicemail: 55–70 words. Full name, Upchurch, and number in the first sentence and again at the end. One hook, one sentence on the work, one ask. Nothing else.
- Never speak the reason-for-outreach label. Never speak the recipient's title.
- Banned, in addition to the shared list: "I'm calling about," "did I catch you at a bad time," "I'm not trying to sell you anything," "touch base," "pick your brain," "circle back."

## Worked example

The sender in this example is from Facility Consulting. Your sender may be from a different department; what transfers is the reasoning pattern, not the pitch. Don't reuse its phrases. The planning block here is identical to the one in the email example; if you edit one, edit both.

Input:
Recipient: Jerry Leafgreen, Director of Facilities and Maintenance, Keller ISD
Reason for outreach: New Bond
Personalization notes: I live in Keller
Research: [$828M bond on the Nov 3 ballot; Prop A $756M for roofing/HVAC/plumbing/electrical; 380+ open HVAC work orders after peaking near 650; ~180 rented spot coolers; classrooms in the 80s; three intermediate schools closing after 2026-27; enrollment down ~4,000 over five years; district site lists his title as Director of Grounds and Maintenance; RWB Consulting Engineers did the MEP audit.]

<planning>
1. Detail: the 180 spot coolers and the 650→380 work-order queue. This is his job. Rejecting campus count and enrollment (institutional), school closures (political, not his win), tax rate and Props B–D (not his lane). Title discrepancy means: no title.
2. Timing problem: Prop A replaces equipment over years. He has to get current equipment through at least two more summers, then keep the new equipment from ending up in the same queue.
3. Work: predictive triage of which units are about to fail so rentals and crews go where they matter; performance assurance on the replacements.
4. Motive: the sender lives in Keller, which is why he's followed this closely. Opens the call as the reason for calling. Kept light so it reads as neighbor, not as voter leverage.
Not naming RWB.
</planning>
<script>
PREP — REFERENCE LIGHTLY
180 rented spot coolers. HVAC tickets peaked ~650, still 380+. Classrooms in the 80s. Prop A ($756M) covers HVAC/roofing/plumbing/electrical, but replacement is years out.

PREP — DON'T BRING UP
RWB (engineer of record; sounds like you're angling to replace them). School closures (political, not his win). Enrollment and tax rate (not his lane). His title (sources disagree).

OPENING
"Jerry, Brian Balzar with Upchurch. I live in Keller, so I've been following the spot cooler situation closer than most. Got 30 seconds?"

THE 30 SECONDS
"You've got a couple hundred rented coolers and a few hundred open tickets, and most of that equipment has to make it through another summer or two before bond money replaces it. We help districts figure out which units are actually about to fail versus which just need attention, so crews and rental dollars go where they matter. I wanted to see if it's worth 20 minutes to compare notes on how you're triaging."

IF ASKED WHAT UPCHURCH DOES
"Energy and building automation firm in DFW. My group does facility consulting: mostly predictive maintenance on aging equipment, and making sure new equipment performs the way it was designed."

DISCOVERY (pick two, let them talk)
- How are you deciding where the coolers go and which tickets get worked first?
- What's the plan for units that fail between now and when Prop A dollars actually land on a campus?
- Is the backlog mostly parts, labor, or units that are just done?

IF "WE'VE GOT ENGINEERS ON THIS"
"Understood. The audit tells you what to replace. This is about the equipment that isn't getting replaced yet."

IF "CALL ME AFTER THE VOTE"
"Happy to. The gap between now and replacement doesn't wait on the vote, though, so I'll check back the week after. Useful if I send one page in the meantime?"

CLOSE
"I'd take 20 minutes at the M&O Center. No deck."

VOICEMAIL
"Jerry, Brian Balzar with Upchurch, [number]. I live in Keller and I've been following the spot cooler situation and the HVAC backlog. We help districts triage which units are about to fail so rental dollars and crews go where they matter. I'd like 20 minutes to compare notes. Brian Balzar, [number]. Thanks."
</script>
